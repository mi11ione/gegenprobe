# Technical report — Gegenprobe: one schema and a converter for Swiss parliamentary answers

**Track 2A — OpenParlData** · Online · Team 444 — Roman (+ Claude Code agents, §9) · Evidence site https://gegenprobe.mi11ion.io · `make run` re-checks the results (§8) · **Maturity:** census and repair lists = *ready to hand over*; nightly job = *deployable prototype* (one real network night under systemd on a CI runner; not yet running at OPD); rules-only converter = *tested prototype*; review mode, whole and virtual affairs = *feasibility study*; schema v0.1 = *draft*.

## 1. Summary

**The two questions.** *Is a common schema feasible?* Yes, with date roles and typed nulls: of 17 fields, **4 are feasible from metadata, 13 partial, 0 need a different concept**; by meaning, 12 are shared, 5 shared with a role split ("answer date" is decided, dispatched or registered), and 3 concepts are body-specific. *Can a converter fill it?* Yes, for what the documents print: shown in depth for the answer date, and for 7 fields of whole affairs, where only the type reaches the unattended bar. Every date and rule value the converter takes from a document carries the page and character span where it is printed; values from OPD metadata are marked `route: metadata` with their source; Apertus's categorical suggestions (type, addressed body, recommendation) are document-level review suggestions without a span; a missing value is a typed null that says why. By default frozen DE/FR/IT rules run without a model; where no rule exists, Apertus points at one of the printed dates as a suggestion a person confirms.
**The cross-parliamentary result the schema enables.** OpenParlData (OPD) publishes the affairs of 89 Swiss parliaments with affairs in scope (it scrapes 90). Of 91,268 interventions whose events record a written government answer, **16,749 (18.4%) have no answer text in OPD** (2024–25: 1,855, 14.3%), each typed by cause; 1,008 are Valais oral answers filed as stubs and 406 question-time answers given in the sitting. In Bern the canton publishes 3,532 answers and OPD attaches 229 (6.5%), although 96.1% are already in OPD and linkable; OPD confirmed the bug and that our relink list is the workaround until its fix ships (correspondence, 10 Oct 2026). Deterministic routes propose repairs for 3,107 of the 16,749; 10,800 written answers need per-parliament source adapters.
**Where Apertus earns its place** (§6). On 62 documents from 15 parliaments the rules never saw (the 29 answer dates come from 7 of them), Apertus finds 24/29 answer dates where the rules find 2/29; on whole affairs it fills the answer date (22/27 → 27/27; held-out replication 20/35 → 31/35). The local 8B finds the dates too (P2-H2 holds for it) but dates many more answerless documents (§3).

## 2. Architecture

1. **Census** (`census/`): streams the OPD exports once (metadata only) into a per-affair *answer-link status* (`attached | agenda | parentless | absent | image_only | oral`), the feasibility matrix and a comparability measure; `make run` recomputes it from a vendored extract.
2. **Repair** (`repair/`): deterministic routes per cause, verified by a triple check (affair number printed, answer heading present, decision date = OPD's answer event), as lists keyed on OPD ids; a nightly job (`deploy/nightly/`) re-runs the joins and raises alerts.
3. **Converter** (`convert/`): (i) OPD metadata and events, (ii) frozen DE/FR/IT anchor rules, (iii) the Apertus closed-set pointer on the residue. Modes: `rules_only` (default, unattended, no model) and `review` (suggestions with page, span and margin, flagged `needs_review`); unknown layouts are flagged `no_rules_for_body`.
4. **Schema and validator** (`schema/`, `validate/`): Pydantic schema v0.1, normative; every field is a value with provenance `{value, role, page, char_start, char_end, quote, route, rule, model_build, θ, margin}` or a *typed null* (`not_in_document | unlinked | image_only | oral | text_failed | present_unanchored | no_rules_for_body | not_applicable`); `QAItem` pairs question *n* with answer *n* (not yet filled). The JSON Schema export cannot express the cross-field rules, so validation uses the Pydantic models.

## 3. Use of Apertus

**Closed-set pointer** (`convert/pointer.py`, prompts `src/prompts/{de,fr,it}`). The user message holds the document (first 12,000 characters) and the question; the assistant turn is prefilled with a JSON prefix (`{"antwort_datum":`); `null` and every distinct date printed in the document are scored as continuations by summed log-probability (exact forced choice via `prompt_logprobs`). Null if `lp(null) − logsumexp(lp(dates)) > θ`, else the best date, mapped back to its span. θ does not transfer across builds (spike F2: a θ fitted on RLVR scores 0.25 on the final build), so it is fitted per build on dev documents and frozen: θ = 10.40 for the Public AI build (AUROC 0.99, leave-one-out 124/127 [93.3, 99.2]). **Why a pointer** (spikes, `results/report/cited_spikes.json`): asked freely, Apertus dates 15/15 (8B) and 14/15 (70B) Bern interventions that have no answer; a null-allowed prompt fixes the 70B (0/15), not the 8B (14/15).

**Which Apertus, which size.** Unless marked 8B, Apertus numbers are the **70B** weights, served by Public AI under the name `swiss-ai/apertus-v1.5-8b` (cited: 760/792 greedy outputs byte-identical to the 70B name; MMLU 84.4% vs 70.8% for the v1 8B route); a system message or prefill to the `-70b` name is answered by Qwen-SEA-LION (3/3), so forced choice goes only to the `-8b` name, the response `model` field is checked on every call, and every cached P1/P2 answer comes from one served build (`results/eval/served_models.json`, D10). The **real 8B** (GGUF Q4_K_M, llama.cpp on one 4-vCPU machine, own θ = 16.73; pre-registered exploratory arms, D2/D3) never returns a date that is not printed. On P1 its pointer and cascade score 112/154 and 119/154 vs 130 and 139 on the 70B (McNemar p = 0.0051 and 3.6 × 10⁻⁵), and it dates 25 of 90 answerless documents (70B: 7); its residue suggestions are right 7/34 (70B 9/16). On P2 (52 of 62 documents reachable from CI, D2-6) it scores rules 33, pointer 35, cascade 33 of 52 (70B pointer 43, cascade 41; 8B vs 70B 2 vs 10 documents, p = 0.039): it finds 15 of 21 answer dates (70B 16, rules 2; P2-H2 holds for it, 13 vs 0, p = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴) but dates 11 of 31 answerless documents (70B 4). Its fast scorer failed the parity gate against the frozen scorer twice (57.8, then 4.47 nats), passed a replacement gate written before any 8B arm ran (bf16 reference, 29/30), and its preset self-check failed (10/12); the 8B numbers are fast-scorer numbers (D9). **Recommendation:** `rules_only` unattended. For review suggestions, the 8B is the measured sovereign option (one CPU machine; it finds the dates but proposes many more on answerless documents, so a reviewer rejects more); the 70B is the accuracy option on infrastructure the operator chooses (bf16 weights about 140 GB, e.g. two 80 GB GPUs; arithmetic, not measured by us).

**Cost** (`results/report/cost_end_to_end.json`, one core, no GPU). Per document, fetching politely, extracting and checking takes about 2.1 s, plus 6.9 s per page where OCR is needed; the rules process 480 documents per second (0.5 CPU-hours for all 902,754 documents in OPD's export). Review mode adds the pointer on the residue (97 of 154 held-out documents): 3.5 calls and 5,905 billed prompt tokens per residue document on the 70B, USD 0.59 per 1,000 residue documents at Public AI's list price; the 8B takes 180 s per document on 4 vCPUs (692 CPU-seconds, 9.3 GB RAM), 50 machine-hours per 1,000. On all of OPD's export at that residue share (about 569k documents, an upper bound) the 8B needs about 28,400 machine-hours on one such machine. At ten times OPD's volume: about 5 CPU-hours for the rules, about USD 3,357 for review mode on the 70B.

## 4. Data and operations

OPD bulk exports (snapshot 6 Oct 2026; OPD's API robots.txt disallows `/v1/`), Bern open data, Fedlex, source PDFs fetched politely (≥ 1.2 s per host; robots.txt per RFC 9309). Both `make run` and the nightly unit run the image read-only (tmpfs `/tmp`), as a non-root uid (10001 when the caller is root), with `--cap-drop ALL` and `no-new-privileges`; PDFs are capped at 50 MB, each pdftotext/tesseract call at 300 s, each document at 600 s, every hit a typed failure row; each event triggers one alert, and failed alerts are re-sent the next night. **One real night** (`results/monitor/night_real_38062530586/`): under systemd on a GitHub-hosted 4-vCPU runner, one network night against OPD's live exports took 83 min (32 of them downloading 198 MB, about 50 the first night's triple check of all 2,508 Bern rows), 28 min of CPU, 2,078 MB peak, 0 alerts, and reproduced the Bern list on live data (2,459 relink, 49 fetch). Inside OPD (`GP_EXPORTS_DIR`) a night with no changed export files took 96.8 s wall on a copied snapshot with 0 bytes of export transfer; a night re-scanning three days of OPD's changes (80.6% of the docs bytes) is about 21.8 min (derived).

## 6. Evaluation: five pre-registrations

**Design.** Every test froze code, prompts, θ and the scorer by sha256 before any label was committed: the LOCK commit precedes the label commit by 6 min 0 s (P1), 2 min 28 s (P2), 3 min 37 s (P3), 4 min 3 s (P4) by commit date (P3b: LOCK5 before the draw), an order GitHub's clock confirms (`results/eval/lock_server_times_all.json`); the annotation files carry no timestamps, so the clock proves order only. Unit: one document's answer date, strict. Arms on identical inputs: **rules**; **pointer** (Apertus on every document); **cascade** (rules, then the pointer on the residue = every review suggestion accepted); **naive** free generation, plain and null-allowed, same weights. **Gold without human labels, by design:** two blind agent annotators (Claude Opus, Sonnet), quotes verified by code, a third breaks ties (order randomised in P2), raw annotations committed, so labels and κ recompute; the committed labels are the record (rerunning the agents need not reproduce them). Non-LLM checks: of 64 P1 answer dates 44 are checkable; OPD's event matches 37 of 44 and 5 of the other 7 are 1–5 days apart (decision vs dispatch); Ticino titles 20/20; P3 answer dates 18/21; the 3 P1 gold dates before OPD's submission date are no gold errors (D11). P2 is thin: 6 of 29 checkable exactly (6/6 agree), 19/29 face at least one non-LLM bound. **Statistics** (post hoc, D10): over the 9 registered confirmatory tests all 4 held conclusions survive Holm (P2-H2 3.8 × 10⁻⁶) and BH; the registered lenient metric changes no registered decision. **Deviations** (`eval/prereg*/DEVIATIONS*.md`): D5/D2-4 naive DD-MM-YYYY re-parse; D6 Branch B as carried out; D7 lock clock; D8 tie-break audit (the Opus tie-breaker sided with the Opus annotator in 36/41 P1 ties, 35 on the recommendation only); D9 8B scorer record; D10 multiplicity, lenient metric, served build; D11 P1 gold audit; D2-5 test population, baselines, θ = 0; D2-6 P2 per parliament on all documents, 8B on P2; D3-5 no P3 dev set; D5-2 a Luzern rule in P3b.

**What the tests say.** Apertus earns its place where layout varies and no rule exists (P2; the answer date in P3/P3b); where structure is regular (P1, P4) rules suffice. **Why the pointer form:** the same Apertus asked freely with a null-allowed prompt is as accurate (P2 re-parsed 54/62 vs 53/62; discordant 1 vs 0, p = 1), but every pointer value is a printed date with page and span (32/32) and its margin orders the review queue (strong band 26/33 right, weak 3/11, post hoc), while the free prompt quotes its date verbatim for 17/32 values and invents dates printed nowhere (1 in P2; the plain prompt 24 in P1).

## 7. Limitations

Answer dates are the field tested in depth; P3/P3b are small (29 + 36 affairs), P4 one commune; no gold set contains federal documents; no human labelled a gold value; the 8B was not run on P3/P3b (on P2, Vaud was unreachable from CI). θ is refitted per build; the pointer sees at most 12,000 characters; spans index our own pdftotext/tesseract text (`--opd-text` maps them onto OPD's).

## 8. Reproducibility

`make run` (Docker, CPU, clean checkout) recomputes the census, the Bern join and triple check, sample conversions with all validator checks, the P1–P4 and P3b scores, the five hypothesis scripts, the 8B analysis and the five LOCK files; re-derives the P1 rules arm from the source PDFs (sha256); recomputes P1 and P2 Apertus decisions from the model cache within a time budget; and re-checks every number in `claims.tsv` against its results file (whole tokens; values of ≤ 3 characters pinned by a context phrase). Locally: rules 154/154, Apertus 154/154 (P1) and 62/62 (P2) identical. In the latest CI run on main whose jobs all passed (38062904003, commit cc37dbd, `results/ci/`): 21/21 replay tests, 142 of 154 rules re-derivations within the budget, all identical; recomputed Apertus decisions matched 129/129 (P1; 25 cache misses from the image's older poppler) and 49/49 (P2; 8 hosts unreachable); 788 claims, 0 failing. That commit precedes the 8B-on-P2 and real-night evidence and this text.
