# Technical report — Gegenprobe: one schema and a converter for Swiss parliamentary answers

**Track 2A — OpenParlData** · Online · Team 444 — Roman (+ Claude Code agents, §7) · Evidence site https://gegenprobe.mi11ion.io · **Maturity:** census and Bern repair lists = *ready to apply* (relinks: one OPD decision, its hash rule); source-adapter lists = *audited*; nightly job = *deployable, two real nights*; rules-only converter = *tested prototype*; review mode, whole and virtual affairs = *feasibility study*; schema v0.1 = *draft for the eCH group*.

## 1. Summary

**The answer.** A common schema for the business of Swiss parliaments works if it carries roles. "Answer date" is the government's decision in some parliaments, the letter or registration date in others and the submission date in a few; schema v0.1 gives every date its role, every value its source (page and character span, or the OpenParlData (OPD) record) and every empty field a typed reason.

1. **Schema.** Five fields are measured to need a role split (answer date, procedural history, type, procedural state, decision outcome), and v0.1 encodes each one. In 7 parliaments OPD's "answer" event is mostly the submission date; median answer times are 112 days where it is the verified decision date, 79 on a government's weekly sitting and 39 for diffuse, publication-like dates. The other 12 fields are defined once in v0.1; a field-by-field meaning test is the next step with the eCH group, set out as P8 for seven of them (`eval/prereg9/`; Table 1).
2. **Census.** Of 91,268 interventions with a written government answer, **92.3%** have answer text after every counted repair, up from 81.6% attached today (85.6% from what OPD already holds, 88.3% after its hash decision for the Bern relinks, 92.3% with the checked fetches). At most 13,150 written answers lack text on their intervention; 2,177 of the "missing" answers were in OPD all along under a heading the census lexicon does not list; in Bern a linking bug, confirmed by OPD (correspondence, 10 Oct 2026), leaves most answers unlinked.
3. **Ready for OPD.** Repairs proposed for **9,720** answers, keyed on OPD ids, among them Bern's 2,421 relinks and 48 fetches with a dry-run SQL plan (and a guarded plan for the tag rows); separately, 554 Bern `begin_date` corrections; per-parliament lists from 50 model-free source adapters (blind audit: 197/200 are the executive's answer, the date wrong or unsupported in 15); 9 OPD vocabulary labels wrong as published (e.g. "Votaziun" on 9,887 affairs); a Fedlex resolver for 29,946 federal documents that store Fedlex's "JavaScript required" page. Schema v0.1 also ships as one standalone file for OPD's workbench (`schema/gegenprobe_schema.py`, pydantic only).
4. **Apertus.** It picks the right printed date where the rules cannot. As a closed-set verifier of the review queue (P7), 95.8% of its promotions carry a date-checked answer date, against 76.2% for the adapters' date rule without a model (+19.6 points, parliament-cluster 95% interval [0.9, 42.8], post hoc); it fills a tier of **214** review-queue answers, and blind annotators judged 120/120 sampled promotions the government's answer. In 15 parliaments nobody wrote rules for it finds 24/29 answer dates (P2), as many as a free prompt, every value a printed date (the latest printed date finds 22 but dates 31 of 33 answerless documents). On submitters both model routes beat the rules (P5: rules 12/40, pointer 24/40, free generation 34/40). Every value it extracts is printed in the document, with page and span, at near free-prompt accuracy (P6: 59 vs 62/68).

## 2. Architecture

**Census** (`census/`) streams the OPD exports once into a per-affair *answer-link status* (`attached | agenda | parentless | absent | image_only | oral`), the feasibility matrix and a comparability measure; a derived layer (`census/corrected.py`) places every registered gap row once the repair routes have looked (Fig. 1). **Repair** (`repair/`): deterministic routes per cause, each row checked (affair number or title, answer heading, date against OPD's answer event), as lists keyed on OPD ids; a nightly job (`deploy/nightly/`) re-runs the Bern route. **Converter** (`convert/`, `qa/`): OPD metadata and events, then frozen DE/FR/IT anchor rules, then the Apertus closed-set pointer on the residue; modes `rules_only` (default, no model) and `review` (suggestions with page, span and margin, flagged `needs_review`). **Schema and validator** (`schema/`, `validate/`): Pydantic schema v0.1, normative; a value carries `{value, role, page, char_start, char_end, quote, route, rule, model_build, θ, margin}`, a null its reason (`not_in_document | unlinked | image_only | oral | text_failed | present_unanchored | no_rules_for_body | not_applicable`); `QAItem` pairs question *n* with its answer passage.

## 3. Use of Apertus

**Closed form only.** Apertus never writes a value. (i) *Verifier* (P7, `repair/adapters/adjudicate.py`): for an adapter review row it answers "does this document print the executive's answer to this intervention (number, title)?" (yes vs no scored after a JSON prefix, θ_A = 8) and picks the answer date among the printed dates or none (θ_B = 10); a row is promoted only if both pass and the date passes the adapters' own date rule. (ii) *Pointer* (`convert/pointer.py`, prompts `src/prompts/{de,fr,it}`): the document (first 12,000 characters) and the question in the user message, the assistant turn prefilled with a JSON prefix, `null` and every distinct printed date scored by summed log-probability; null if `lp(null) − logsumexp(lp(dates)) > θ`. Code locates every span (`pointer.locate`: the occurrence nearest an answer anchor, else the first). θ does not transfer across builds, so it is fitted per build on dev documents and frozen (θ = 10.40, AUROC 0.99). The same form picks labels and, in `qa/`, the question a paragraph answers, the recommendation sentence or the submitter's printed name. **Why closed:** asked freely, Apertus dates 15/15 (8B) and 14/15 (70B) Bern interventions that have no answer. **Design:** Apertus suggestions go to a reviewer; rules run unattended.

**Which model.** Evaluation used **Apertus v1.5 70B weights served by Public AI** under the name `swiss-ai/apertus-v1.5-8b` (inferred from the served fingerprint `tp8-pp2-…` and 760/792 greedy outputs byte-identical to the `-70b` name); the response `model` is checked on every call. The **8B** (`swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B`, a community 4-bit GGUF, sha256-pinned) runs locally on CPU with its own θ = 16.73 (exploratory): on P2's unseen parliaments it finds 15 of 21 answer dates (70B 16), and the registered P2-H2 test over the rules gives 13 vs 0; its two-stage reader v2 cuts dates on answerless documents from 18/34 to 5/34 (P6; v2 51/67 vs v1 48/67; rules then v2 55/67 vs rules 54/67). **Recommendation:** `rules_only` unattended; for suggestions the 8B where no text may leave the machine, the 70B for accuracy on infrastructure the operator chooses. Review mode stops (exit 3) on a served build θ was not fitted on.

**Cost** (`results/report/cost_end_to_end.json`, one core, no GPU). Fetch, extraction and checks about 2.1 s per document plus 6.9 s per OCR page; the rules process 480 documents per second (0.5 CPU-hours for all 902,754 documents in OPD's export). Review mode: 3.5 calls and 5,905 billed prompt tokens per residue document on the 70B, USD 0.59 per 1,000 residue documents; P7's verifier on the whole queue cost USD 0.50. The 8B takes 180 s per document on 4 vCPUs. For the whole export (about 569k residue documents, an upper bound) review mode would cost about USD 336 on the 70B or 28,400 machine-hours on the 8B; at ten times OPD's volume the rules need about 5 CPU-hours.

## 4. Data and operations

**Data.** OPD bulk exports (snapshot 6 Oct 2026; CC BY 4.0, "Source: OpenParlData.ch"; OPD's API robots.txt disallows `/v1/`), Bern open data, Fedlex, parliament sites, fetched politely (≥ 1.2 s per host and process). `data/` and `results/` are CDLA-Permissive-2.0 and carry OPD's attribution. **Sovereignty.** No third-party processors; read-only requests to public sources: OPD's exports, Bern's open data, Fedlex, document hosts and the parliaments' sites (source adapters, nightly canaries), Hugging Face once for the pinned 8B; a review-model endpoint (Public AI, or the local 8B on CPU, where no text leaves the machine) and an alert relay only if the operator sets them. **Operations.** `make run` and the nightly unit run the image read-only, as a non-root uid, with `--cap-drop ALL`; PDFs are capped at 50 MB and 600 s. On GitHub runners: one real night under systemd (83 min, Bern list reproduced on live data), then **two consecutive real nights in the recommended Docker setup** (Actions run 38098822336): night 1 took 86 min and streamed 6.4 GB; night 2 took 6.6 min (12 bodies re-scanned), 0 alerts; the ledger-status bug that night 2 exposed is fixed and covered by a two-night test. **Ready to apply:** the dry-run plans are keyed on OPD ids; the only open decision is OPD's hash rule for relinks. **robots.txt:** we found, audited and fixed our own robots.txt bug; every fetch path now fails closed (disclosure: `docs/incidents.md`).

## 6. Evaluation: eight pre-registrations

**Design.** Each test froze code, prompts and θ by sha256 before any label was committed (P1's hypothesis script followed its labels, D4). Unit (P1, P2, P6): one document's answer date, strict. Arms on identical inputs: **rules**; **pointer**; **cascade** (rules, then the pointer on the residue); **naive** free generation, plain and null-allowed; trivial baselines. **Gold, no human labelling by design:** two blind agent annotators (Claude Opus, Sonnet), a third breaks ties, quotes verified by code, raw files committed; all 84 annotator sessions ended before GitHub received their labels (`results/eval/annotation_sessions.json`). P1's rules were committed with its sample (D12), so P2 is the clean out-of-sample test of the rules.

**P6 — reader v2** (an answer-present gate, then the gold guide's decision-before-letter-date rule). No registered hypothesis held: v2 vs free prompting 1:4 (p = 0.38); v2 dates 5/34 answerless documents (upper bound 30.1% vs 15%), all 5 on documents that contain an answer without a qualifying date; v2 vs v1 5:2 (p = 0.45). **P7 — Apertus as verifier of the review queue** (`eval/prereg8/`, LOCK8 before the draw; replayed from `data/p7/`). Test frame: 478 eligible review rows outside every audit; 190 blind packets, gold A1 Opus / A2 Sonnet. H1 (primary): promotion precision 120/120, Wilson lower bound 96.9% ≥ 0.90: **holds**. H4: dated precision 95.8% vs 73.2% for a trivial exact-date match, +22.7 points [10.2, 34.3]: **supported**. H3: the rows free generation with a printed-date filter adds are 28/30 right: **not supported**. Base rate: 86.7% of eligible queue rows are answers, but only 40.9% are answers whose own date passes the rule, so the gain is the date check. Post hoc (`results/p7/p7_posthoc_r8.json`, `p7_cluster_r9.json`): the date rule without a model, on any printed date of the same closed set, reaches 76.2% date-checked, P7 +19.6 points [8.4, 30.9] (parliament-cluster [0.9, 42.8]); keeping Apertus's yes/no gate and letting the rule choose gives 74.9%, so the date choice is worth +20.9 points [5.2, 33.1] (cluster [0, 47.4]), the gate 1.1 on is-answer; a model-free date match is also right on is-answer (146/146); without Frauenfeld (109 of 214 promotions) 48/48. Applied to the whole queue, 214 of 917 rows go to the tier `promoted_by_apertus`, listed next to the 6,613 counted rows as a pre-sorted batch for review.

**What the tests say.** Apertus earns its place where layout varies and no rule exists (P2, P3) and as a closed-set verifier with a date check (P7). **Provenance by construction:** no arm that picks from printed dates returned a date printed nowhere, on P1, P2 or P6, and every value has a location, at accuracy close to the same Apertus asked freely with null allowed (P2 53 vs 54 of 62; P6 59 vs 62 of 68; P7-H3); a printed-date filter on the free prompt gives the same no-invention guarantee, the closed set gives it by construction, with a page, span and margin. **Null safety** is left to the reviewer: the registered null-safety tests on the 70B failed (P1-H1, P2-H3, P6-H2), which is why the default runs no model.

## 7. Limitations and next steps, reproducibility, declarations

**Next steps.** Answer dates are tested in depth and Q/A pairs on 40 affairs; tables, annexes and images inside answers are the next extraction target; P4 covers one commune, P6 has no Italian document, no gold set contains federal documents; spans index our own pdftotext/tesseract text (`--opd-text` maps them onto OPD's). With OPD: the Bern, Fedlex and adapter lists and the nightly job on its side, its hash decision, a spot check of the review rows; with the eCH group: the field-by-field meaning test and schema v0.2 (date roles, `QAItem`, five gaps). **Reproducibility.** `make run` (Docker, CPU, clean checkout) recomputes the census and the Bern join, re-derives the P1 rules arm from the source PDFs and the P1/P2 Apertus decisions from the model cache, replays the registered tests from committed files (P7 included), and recomputes every number in `claims.tsv` from its results file, failing if a document prints anything else. **Declarations.** Claude Code agents (Anthropic) wrote most code, documentation and the gold labels; numbers are checked by code. Public AI (may route outside Switzerland) served evaluation only. Report CC-BY-4.0; standards in `docs/mapping.md`.
